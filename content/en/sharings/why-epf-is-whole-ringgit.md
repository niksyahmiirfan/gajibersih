If you have ever multiplied your salary by 11% and got a slightly different number from your payslip, you have met the **KWSP Third Schedule**. EPF is not worked out as an exact percentage for most salaries. Instead, it uses wage bands.

## How the wage bands work

The Third Schedule splits salaries into small ranges:

- Up to RM5,000, the bands are RM20 wide.
- From RM5,000 to RM20,000, they are RM100 wide.
- Above RM20,000, EPF is a straight percentage of your actual salary.

For each band, the contribution is calculated on the **top of the band**, and any cents are **rounded up to the next ringgit**. That is why most EPF amounts are whole ringgit.

## Some examples

Using the standard rates for employees under 60 (11% from you, 13% from your employer on salaries up to RM5,000):

- **RM3,000**: you pay RM330 and your employer pays RM390.
- **RM4,990**: this falls in the band that tops out at RM5,000, so you pay RM550 and your employer pays RM650, the same as on exactly RM5,000.

## What happens just above RM5,000

Above RM5,000, the employer rate drops from 13% to 12%, and the bands get wider. On a salary of RM5,000.01, the band tops out at RM5,100. You pay RM561 and your employer pays RM612. So a small raise across RM5,000 can slightly *reduce* what your employer puts into your EPF, while your own share goes up.

Together, you and your employer put in 24% of your band on salaries up to RM5,000, and 23% above it.

- **Your payslip is probably right.** Small differences from a quick 11% sum are expected.
